How it works: For each segment, the field-average pace ratio (segment_pace / overall_pace) across 13 finishers gives a difficulty multiplier — e.g. the Ouray downhill averages 0.52× overall pace (easy), the Telluride→Kroger climb averages 1.39× (hard). Dividing Nick's segment pace by this multiplier shows how he performed relative to what the terrain and course position demand. A flat adjusted line = perfect pacing for the course. A rising line = fading. This uses 2026 field data applied to all CW years (same course, same difficulty template). CCW years use reversed-section mapping where physical trails match.

Key Insights

Career Arc: Started at 21 with an impressive 5th place. Steady improvement through his 20s, peaking at 30 with the 27:14 PR (16:15/mi). After a plateau in the 30-31h range through his 30s, posted a strong 29:53 at age 38 (2026) — his second-fastest Hardrock ever and fastest CW finish.

Direction Matters: Every CCW finish was faster than the nearest CW finish by 1-3 hours. Part of this is the course profile — the CCW direction tackles the steepest climbs earlier when legs are fresher.

Aid Station Discipline: The fastest year (2017) spent only 35 minutes in aid stations across the entire 100 miles. The slowest (2016) spent 90 minutes. Quick transitions are a clear performance indicator.

Consistency: The 2026 race (29:53) broke a decade-long CW plateau — the previous CW best was 30:34 (2024). Efficient aid station transitions (27m total) and strong pacing through the mid-race climbs drove the improvement.

Youthful Start: Nick's first Hardrock at age 21 (2008) resulted in 5th place and 31:07 — one of the strongest debuts in race history for that age group.
